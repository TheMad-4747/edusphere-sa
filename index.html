📘 English FAL — Grade 10: Comprehension & Language

Comprehension & Language

Grade 10 English FAL 25 Marks
Question 1 (15 marks)

Read the passage below and answer the questions that follow:

The morning sun cast long shadows across the dusty road as Thabo made his way to school. He walked with determination, his worn school shoes kicking up small clouds of red dust with each step. Despite the challenges he faced at home, Thabo never missed a day of school. His mother had always told him that education was the key to a better future, and he held onto those words like a lifeline. As he approached the school gates, he could hear the laughter of his classmates and the familiar ringing of the school bell. Today was an important day – the day of the mathematics competition, and Thabo had spent weeks preparing for it.

1.1. Describe the setting of the passage. (3 marks)

1.2. What does the passage reveal about Thabo's character? (4 marks)

1.3. Explain the metaphor "he held onto those words like a lifeline." (4 marks)

1.4. Identify and explain the mood created in the first paragraph. (4 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

Rewrite the following sentences according to the instructions:

2.1. "Thabo walked to school." (Change into the passive voice.) (2 marks)

2.2. "The sun cast long shadows." (Add an adjective to describe the shadows.) (2 marks)

2.3. "Thabo never missed a day of school." (Change into a question.) (2 marks)

2.4. "The school bell rang." (Add a subordinate clause.) (2 marks)

2.5. "Thabo had spent weeks preparing." (Begin with 'Weeks had been spent...') (2 marks)

✅ Memorandum

1.1
Setting (3 marks):
• Rural/semi-rural area (dusty road, red dust)
• Morning time (morning sun, long shadows)
• School environment (school gates, school bell, classmates)
1.2
Thabo's character (4 marks):
• Determined/persevering (walks with determination, never misses school)
• Resilient (faces challenges at home but persists)
• Dedicated/hardworking (spent weeks preparing for competition)
• Values education (holds onto mother's words)
1.3
Metaphor (4 marks):
"Held onto those words like a lifeline" compares his mother's words to a lifeline (a rope that saves someone from drowning). This suggests that without education/his mother's guidance, Thabo would be lost or in danger. It shows how deeply he values her advice and how education is his only hope for escaping poverty.
1.4
Mood (4 marks):
The mood is one of quiet determination and hope. The morning sun suggests a new beginning, while the dusty road and worn shoes suggest hardship. However, Thabo's determination and the sound of the school bell create a sense of optimism and purpose.
2.1
Passive voice: School was walked to by Thabo.
2.2
With adjective: The sun cast long, dark shadows.
2.3
Question: Did Thabo ever miss a day of school?
2.4
With subordinate clause: The school bell rang as the learners entered the classroom.
2.5
Begin with 'Weeks had been spent...': Weeks had been spent by Thabo preparing for the mathematics competition.
FINAL ANSWERS: All answers above

➗ Mathematics — Grade 10: Algebraic Equations & Exponents

Algebraic Equations & Exponents

Grade 10 Mathematics 25 Marks
Question 1 (8 marks)

Solve for x: 2x+1 + 2x = 48

Question 2 (10 marks)

Simplify: (3x × 9x-1) / 27x

Question 3 (7 marks)

Solve for x: 52x - 6 × 5x + 5 = 0

✅ Memorandum — Step-by-Step

1
Q1 Step 1: Identify common base
2x+1 = 2x × 21 = 2 × 2x
2x+1 = 2 × 2x
2
Q1 Step 2: Substitute
2 × 2x + 2x = 48
2 × 2x + 2x = 48
3
Q1 Step 3: Factor out 2x
2x(2 + 1) = 48
2x × 3 = 48 → 2x = 16
4
Q1 Step 4: Solve
16 = 24, so x = 4
2x = 24 → x = 4
5
Q2 Step 1: Convert to base 3
9 = 32, 27 = 33
9x-1 = (32)x-1 = 32x-2
27x = (33)x = 33x
6
Q2 Step 2: Simplify
3x × 32x-2 = 33x-2
33x-2 / 33x = 3-2 = 1/9
7
Q3 Step 1: Let k = 5x
52x = (5x)2 = k2
k2 - 6k + 5 = 0
8
Q3 Step 2: Factor
(k - 5)(k - 1) = 0 → k = 5 or k = 1
5x = 51 → x = 1
5x = 50 → x = 0
FINAL ANSWERS: Q1: x = 4 | Q2: 1/9 | Q3: x = 1 or x = 0

➗ Mathematics — Grade 10: Functions

Linear, Quadratic & Exponential Functions

Grade 10 Mathematics 35 Marks
Question 1 (12 marks)

Given f(x) = 2x + 3 and g(x) = x² - 4:

a) Calculate f(-2). (2 marks)

b) Find x if g(x) = 5. (3 marks)

c) Determine the coordinates of the turning point of g(x). (3 marks)

d) Sketch the graphs of f and g on the same set of axes, showing all intercepts. (4 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

The graph of h(x) = ax passes through the point (2, 9).

a) Determine the value of a. (2 marks)

b) Calculate h(-1). (2 marks)

c) Determine the equation of the asymptote of h(x). (2 marks)

d) Sketch the graph of h(x), showing the asymptote and intercepts. (4 marks)

Question 3 (13 marks)

Given the quadratic function p(x) = -x² + 4x + 5:

a) Determine the x-intercepts. (3 marks)

b) Determine the y-intercept. (1 mark)

c) Determine the axis of symmetry. (2 marks)

d) Determine the turning point. (2 marks)

e) Sketch the graph showing all key features. (3 marks)

f) For which values of x is p(x) > 0? (2 marks)

✅ Memorandum — Step-by-Step

1a
f(-2)
f(x) = 2x + 3
f(-2) = 2(-2) + 3 = -4 + 3 = -1
1b
g(x) = 5
x² - 4 = 5
x² = 9 → x = ±3
1c
Turning point
For y = ax² + bx + c, x = -b/(2a)
x = -0/(2×1) = 0
y = (0)² - 4 = -4
Turning point: (0; -4)
2a
Find a
h(2) = 9, so a² = 9
a = 3 (a > 0 for growth)
2b
h(-1)
h(x) = 3x
h(-1) = 3-1 = 1/3
2c
Asymptote
As x → -∞, 3x → 0
Horizontal asymptote: y = 0
3a
x-intercepts
-x² + 4x + 5 = 0 → x² - 4x - 5 = 0
(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0 → x = 5 or x = -1
x-intercepts: (-1; 0) and (5; 0)
3b
y-intercept
p(0) = -(0)² + 4(0) + 5 = 5
y-intercept: (0; 5)
3c
Axis of symmetry
x = -b/(2a) = -4/(2×(-1)) = 2
x = 2
3d
Turning point
x = 2, p(2) = -(2)² + 4(2) + 5 = -4 + 8 + 5 = 9
Maximum turning point: (2; 9)
3f
p(x) > 0
Parabola opens downward, above x-axis between intercepts
-1 < x < 5
FINAL ANSWERS: 1a) -1 | 1b) x=±3 | 1c) (0;-4) | 2a) a=3 | 2b) 1/3 | 3a) (-1;0),(5;0) | 3f) -1<x<5

➗ Mathematics — Grade 10: Number Patterns & Sequences

Arithmetic & Geometric Sequences

Grade 10 Mathematics 30 Marks
Question 1 (10 marks)

Consider the arithmetic sequence: 5, 9, 13, 17, ...

a) Determine the general term Tn. (3 marks)

b) Calculate the 20th term. (2 marks)

c) Which term is equal to 101? (3 marks)

d) Calculate the sum of the first 25 terms. (2 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

Consider the geometric sequence: 3, 6, 12, 24, ...

a) Determine the common ratio. (1 mark)

b) Determine the general term Tn. (2 marks)

c) Calculate the 8th term. (2 marks)

d) Determine the first term that exceeds 1000. (3 marks)

e) Calculate the sum of the first 10 terms. (2 marks)

Question 3 (10 marks)

The 3rd term of an arithmetic sequence is 14 and the 7th term is 30. Find:

a) The first term and common difference. (5 marks)

b) The sum of the first 15 terms. (3 marks)

c) How many terms must be added to get a sum of 440? (2 marks)

✅ Memorandum — Step-by-Step

1a
General term
a = 5, d = 4
Tn = 5 + (n-1)(4) = 4n + 1
1b
20th term
T20 = 4(20) + 1 = 81
1c
Find n when Tn = 101
4n + 1 = 101 → 4n = 100 → n = 25
1d
Sum of first 25 terms
S25 = 25/2[2(5) + (24)(4)] = 12.5 × 106 = 1325
2a
Common ratio
r = 6/3 = 2
2b
General term
Tn = 3 × 2n-1
2c
8th term
T8 = 3 × 27 = 3 × 128 = 384
2d
First term > 1000
3 × 2n-1 > 1000 → 2n-1 > 333.33
28 = 256, 29 = 512 → n-1 = 9 → n = 10
2e
Sum of first 10 terms
S10 = 3(210-1)/(2-1) = 3(1023) = 3069
3a
Find a and d
T3 = a + 2d = 14, T7 = a + 6d = 30
Subtract: 4d = 16 → d = 4
a + 8 = 14 → a = 6
3b
Sum of first 15 terms
S15 = 15/2[2(6) + (14)(4)] = 7.5 × 68 = 510
3c
Find n when Sn = 440
n/2[12 + (n-1)(4)] = 440 → n(2n+4) = 440
2n² + 4n - 440 = 0 → n² + 2n - 220 = 0
n = (-2 + √884)/2 ≈ 13.86 → n = 14
FINAL ANSWERS: Q1: Tn=4n+1, T20=81, n=25, S25=1325 | Q2: r=2, Tn=3×2n-1, T8=384, n=10, S10=3069 | Q3: a=6, d=4, S15=510, n=14

⚗️ Physical Sciences — Grade 10: Mechanics

Forces & Motion

Grade 10 Physical Sciences 30 Marks
Question 1 (10 marks)

A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 5 seconds. Calculate:

a) The acceleration of the car. (3 marks)

b) The distance traveled during this time. (4 marks)

c) The velocity of the car after 3 seconds. (3 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

A 5 kg block is pulled along a horizontal surface by a force of 20 N at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.2. Calculate:

a) The normal force. (3 marks)

b) The frictional force. (3 marks)

c) The acceleration of the block. (4 marks)

Question 3 (10 marks)

Explain Newton's three laws of motion and give a real-life example for each.

✅ Memorandum — Step-by-Step

1a
Acceleration
v = u + at, u = 0, v = 20 m/s, t = 5 s
a = (v - u)/t = (20 - 0)/5 = 4 m/s²
1b
Distance
s = ut + ½at²
s = 0 + ½(4)(5)² = 2 × 25 = 50 m
1c
Velocity at t=3s
v = u + at = 0 + 4(3) = 12 m/s
2a
Normal force
Vertical forces: N + Fsin30° = mg
N = mg - Fsin30° = 5(9.8) - 20(0.5) = 49 - 10 = 39 N
2b
Frictional force
f = μN = 0.2 × 39 = 7.8 N
2c
Acceleration
Fnet = Fcos30° - f = 20(0.866) - 7.8 = 17.32 - 7.8 = 9.52 N
a = Fnet/m = 9.52/5 = 1.90 m/s²
3
Newton's Laws
1st Law: An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force. Example: A book on a table stays still until pushed.

2nd Law: F = ma. The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass. Example: Pushing a shopping cart — harder push = more acceleration; heavier cart = less acceleration.

3rd Law: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Example: When you jump, you push down on the ground, and the ground pushes you up.
FINAL ANSWERS: Q1: a) 4 m/s² b) 50 m c) 12 m/s | Q2: a) 39 N b) 7.8 N c) 1.90 m/s²

⚗️ Physical Sciences — Grade 10: Waves, Sound & Light

Transverse & Longitudinal Waves

Grade 10 Physical Sciences 30 Marks
Question 1 (10 marks)

A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz and a wavelength of 0.4 m.

a) Calculate the speed of the wave. (3 marks)

b) If the frequency is doubled, what happens to the wavelength? (3 marks)

c) Calculate the period of the wave. (2 marks)

d) How many complete waves pass a point in 5 seconds? (2 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

A sound wave travels through air at 340 m/s. The sound has a frequency of 680 Hz.

a) Calculate the wavelength of the sound wave. (3 marks)

b) Explain why sound cannot travel through a vacuum. (3 marks)

c) A person stands 680 m from a cliff and shouts. How long until they hear the echo? (4 marks)

Question 3 (10 marks)

Light travels from air into glass. The speed of light in air is 3 × 10⁸ m/s and in glass is 2 × 10⁸ m/s.

a) Calculate the refractive index of glass. (3 marks)

b) If the angle of incidence in air is 40°, calculate the angle of refraction in glass using Snell's Law. (4 marks)

c) Explain what happens to the wavelength of light when it enters glass. (3 marks)

✅ Memorandum — Step-by-Step

1a
Wave speed
v = f × λ
v = 50 × 0.4 = 20 m/s
1b
Effect of doubling frequency
v is constant, so if f doubles, λ halves.
λ = v/f = 20/100 = 0.2 m (halved from 0.4 m)
1c
Period
T = 1/f = 1/50 = 0.02 s = 20 ms
1d
Number of waves
Number = f × t = 50 × 5 = 250 complete waves
2a
Wavelength
λ = v/f = 340/680 = 0.5 m
2b
Sound in vacuum
Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel. It propagates by vibrating particles. In a vacuum, there are no particles to vibrate, so sound cannot travel.
2c
Echo time
Total distance = 2 × 680 = 1360 m
t = distance/speed = 1360/340 = 4 seconds
3a
Refractive index
n = c/v = (3 × 10⁸)/(2 × 10⁸) = 1.5
3b
Snell's Law
n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂
1 × sin(40°) = 1.5 × sin(θ₂)
sin(θ₂) = 0.6428/1.5 = 0.4285
θ₂ = sin⁻¹(0.4285) = 25.4°
3c
Wavelength in glass
When light enters a denser medium, its speed decreases but frequency stays constant. Since v = fλ, if v decreases and f is constant, λ must decrease. The wavelength is compressed.
FINAL ANSWERS: Q1: a) 20 m/s b) Halved to 0.2 m c) 0.02 s d) 250 | Q2: a) 0.5 m c) 4 s | Q3: a) 1.5 b) 25.4°

🗣️ Sepedi — Grade 10: Gramotiki ya Sepedi

Gramotiki le Go Hlathwa

Grade 10 Sepedi 30 Marks
Potšišo 1 (Maka 10)

Tšhupetšo ya Gramotiki: Tsenya mafoko a latelago ka tsela ya maleba ka go šomiša "go" le "mo".

1.1. Ke ___ (ya) gae. (2)

1.2. Rena re ___ (bina) pina. (2)

1.3. Bana ba ___ (raka) mpsa. (2)

1.4. O ___ (ja) dijo. (2)

1.5. Re ___ (bala) puku. (2)

Potšišo 2 (Maka 10)

Ditlhaelelo: Ngwala ditlhaelelo tše nne tša lentšu "thuto".

Potšišo 3 (Maka 10)

Kanegelo: Ngwala kanegelo ka botlokotsebe ka ga letšatši la gago la matswalo. Šomiša mafoko a 150-200.

✅ Memorandum — Dikarabo tše Rulaganyegilego

1.1
Ke ya gae.
"Go ya" ke infinitive. Ge re šomiša "ke", re swanetše go šomiša present tense.
1.2
Rena re bina pina.
"Re" (first person plural) + verb stem "bina" = present tense.
1.3
Bana ba raka mpsa.
"Ba" (third person plural) + verb stem "raka" = present tense. Subject concord ya Class 2 ke "ba".
1.4
O ja dijo.
"O" (third person singular, Class 1) + verb stem "ja" = present tense.
1.5
Re bala puku.
"Re" (first person plural) + verb stem "bala" = present tense.
2
Ditlhaelelo tša "thuto":
1. Thuto ke tlhago ya go ithuta le go rutwa.
2. Thuto e ka bago kalafo ya tlhago ya setšhaba.
3. Thuto e ka bago thero ya dithuto tše di rutwago sekolo.
4. Thuto e ka bago maitekolo a go ithuta dintlha tša bodutu.
3
Mekgwa ya go ngwala kanegelo:
1. Thoma ka peakanyo (introduction)
2. Mmele (body) - bolela ka dilo tše di diregilego
3. Mafelelo (conclusion)
4. Mafoko a 150-200
KARABO: 1.1 ya, 1.2 bina, 1.3 raka, 1.4 ja, 1.5 bala

Potšišo 3 (Maka 10)

Go Ngwala: Ngwala poema ya gago ka ga "Bokamoso bja ka". Šomiša mafoko a go swana le dikgopolo. (Mafoko a 100-150)

✅ Memorandum — Dikarabo tše Rulaganyegilego

1.1
Magoro a mafsa:
• Lefsa le tšhaba bokamoso
• Lefsa le tšhaba go hlokomela bophelo
• Lefsa le tšhaba go ba le sebete
1.2
Maikutlo a mokišiši:
• Maikutlo a mafsa (mixed feelings)
• O tšhaba bokamoso bjo bo sa tsebego
• O sa kwešiše gore a itumele goba a tšhabe
1.3
Mohuta:
Ke poema ya go swana (lyrical poem/refrain poem). Ka baka la gore e šomiša mafoko a go swana ("Lefsa le leša le tšhaba" a boelwa gape).
1.4
Dikgopolo tše nne:
1. Lefsa le tšhaba bokamoso
2. Lefsa le tšhaba go hlokomela
3. Lefsa le tšhaba go ba le sebete
4. Lefsa le nyaka go tseba gore le tla fihla bjang

🏛️ History — Grade 10: The Road to Democracy in South Africa

The Road to Democracy

Grade 10 History 30 Marks
Question 1 (10 marks)

Explain the significance of the following events in the struggle against apartheid:

a) The Sharpeville Massacre (1960) (3 marks)

b) The Soweto Uprising (1976) (4 marks)

c) The release of Nelson Mandela (1990) (3 marks)

Question 2 (10 marks)

Discuss the role of the ANC and PAC in the liberation struggle. Compare their strategies and approaches.

Question 3 (10 marks)

Explain how international pressure contributed to the end of apartheid. Include specific examples of sanctions and boycotts.

✅ Memorandum

1a
Sharpeville Massacre (1960):
• 69 protesters killed, 180 wounded during peaceful protest against pass laws
• Led to banning of ANC and PAC; shift to armed struggle
• International condemnation and beginning of isolation of SA regime
1b
Soweto Uprising (1976):
• Students protested against Afrikaans as medium of instruction
• Police opened fire; Hector Pieterson killed (photo became iconic)
• Sparked nationwide protests and international outrage
• Led to formation of COSAS, AZAPO, and renewed resistance
• Significant turning point in the struggle
1c
Release of Mandela (1990):
• Released after 27 years in prison on 11 February 1990
• Symbolized the beginning of the end of apartheid
• Led to negotiations between ANC and NP government
• Mandela became president in 1994
2
ANC vs PAC:
ANC: Multi-racial, broad-based, initially non-violent (Defiance Campaign, Freedom Charter), later armed struggle (MK after 1960). Led by Mandela, Tambo, Sisulu.

PAC: Africanist, broke away from ANC in 1959 under Robert Sobukwe. More militant, focused on Black consciousness. Organized Sharpeville protest. Believed in "Africa for Africans."

Comparison: ANC was more inclusive and patient; PAC was more radical and immediate. Both contributed to liberation but had different ideologies.
3
International Pressure:
• Sanctions: Economic sanctions limited trade, investment, and loans. Oil embargo made fuel expensive.
• Sports boycott: SA banned from Olympics (1964-1992), FIFA, rugby, cricket. This isolated SA culturally.
• Divestment: Multinationals withdrew (Barclays, GM).
• Anti-apartheid movements: AAM in Britain, worldwide campaigns.
• UN: Condemned apartheid, imposed arms embargo (1977).
• These pressures weakened the SA economy and government, forcing negotiations.
FINAL ANSWERS: All answers above
