📝 Test Your Knowledge

Lesson 01 Quiz — Barrett's Taxonomy Aligned

Lesson 01: What is English FAL?

4 Sample Questions • DBE NSC Style

Question 1 (2 marks) — Low Order
According to the lesson, what does the acronym FAL stand for in the context of South African education?
A First Additional Language
B Foreign Applied Language
C Formal Academic Learning
D First Advanced Level
✅ Correct! First Additional Language
FAL stands for "First Additional Language." This is explicitly stated in the lesson when Mr. Chauke asks the class and Naledi answers correctly. This is a Barrett's Level 1 (Literal) question — the answer is directly stated in the text.
Question 2 (2 marks) — Low Order
List two of the four language skills that form the foundation of English FAL.
A Listening and Speaking
B Memorising and Reciting
C Drawing and Calculating
D Running and Jumping
✅ Correct! Listening and Speaking
The four language skills are: Listening, Speaking, Reading, and Writing. Mr. Chauke explicitly names these when he says English FAL is "built on four skills. Think of them as four legs of a table." This is a Barrett's Level 1 (Literal) — direct recall from text.
Question 3 (2 marks) — Middle Order
When Mr. Chauke says English FAL is a "superpower," what does this metaphor suggest about the value of learning English FAL?
A It gives learners special abilities and opportunities beyond their home language
B It makes learners better than those who only speak one language
C It is a difficult subject that only smart learners can pass
D It is a compulsory subject that everyone must take
✅ Correct! Special abilities and opportunities
The "superpower" metaphor suggests English FAL gives learners abilities they would not otherwise have — access to universities, jobs, and global communication. Naledi says: "It is a superpower, Clement. But most people do not know that yet." This requires inferring implied meaning — Barrett's Level 3 (Inference).
Question 4 (2 marks) — High Order
Do you think Mr. Chauke's teaching approach — using metaphors and real-life examples — is an effective way to introduce English FAL? Substantiate your answer.
A Yes — it makes abstract concepts relatable, as shown by Clement's and Lebo's changed attitudes
B No — it wastes time that could be spent on actual grammar rules
C Yes — but only for learners who already like English
D No — metaphors confuse learners who need direct explanations
✅ Correct! Yes, it makes concepts relatable
Mr. Chauke's approach is effective because: (1) Clement moves from confusion to understanding, (2) Lebo becomes engaged when he sees relevance to his dream job, (3) The "superpower" metaphor is memorable. This is Barrett's Level 4 (Evaluation) — making a judgment with evidence from the text.
